# Topic questions

Some topics always hide decisions. When an item touches a topic below, go through every question in it. Ask it, or mark it "already answered" or "not relevant" with a reason. Ask the questions that matter most first.

Each question comes with an example. Change the names and numbers to fit the person's business. Each line is one question; ask them one at a time.

## Personal details and history

**Watch for:** name, email, phone, address, date of birth, health information, account, profile, history, "the system remembers".

**If nobody decides:** we keep data we must delete, or delete data we must keep, and break privacy law either way.

- **How long do we keep it?** Example: Anna stops being a client in 2026. Do we still keep her details in 2030?
- **Can a person ask us to delete it?** Example: Anna emails "Please delete everything about me." What happens?
- **Hidden but kept, or gone forever?** Example: after deletion, can a manager still see Anna's old bookings in a report?
- **What happens to linked information?** Example: Anna's account is removed. What happens to her bookings, invoices, and test results?
- **Who can see it?** Example: can a receptionist see Anna's test results, or only her doctor?
- **Is it sensitive?** Health, religion, or sexuality need extra care. Example: does the booking form ask why Anna is coming in?

## Money

**Watch for:** price, discount, voucher, invoice, payment, refund, fee, subscription, VAT, tax, balance.

**If nobody decides:** bills come out a penny wrong, refunds are argued case by case, and the accounts do not match.

- **Which currency?** Example: can a client in Ireland pay in euros?
- **Which exchange rate, from which day?** Example: Anna pays in euros in March and gets a refund in April. Which day's rate applies?
- **How do we round?** Example: 10% off £19.99 is £17.991. Do we charge £17.99 or £18.00?
- **Which price counts?** Example: Anna books at £100. The price rises to £120 before her appointment. Which does she pay?
- **When is the price fixed?** When she adds it to the basket, when she books, or when she pays?
- **Do discounts add up?** Example: a 10% voucher plus a 20% company discount. Is that 30% off, one after the other, or only the bigger one?
- **How do refunds work?** Example: Anna cancels the day before. Full refund, part refund, or none?
- **How do we correct a bill?** Example: Anna was charged for two tests but got one. What should the corrected bill show?
- **Who pays?** Example: Anna's employer pays for the test, but Anna adds an extra. Who pays for the extra?

## Several people working on the same thing

**Watch for:** team, shared, assign, owner, manager, editor, viewer, colleague, "anyone in the clinic".

**If nobody decides:** people overwrite each other's work, or see things they should not.

- **Who can change it, and who can only look?** Example: can a receptionist change the price, or only the manager?
- **Who sees what?** Example: does Clinic A see Clinic B's bookings?
- **What if the owner leaves?** Example: Ben created 40 bookings, then leaves the company. Who now owns them?
- **What if two people change it at the same moment?** Example: two receptionists give the last free slot to two different patients. Who wins?

## Talking to another company's system

**Watch for:** sends to, gets from, imports, exports, connects to, a named outside company or product, lab, courier, the company that takes card payments.

**If nobody decides:** one broken outside system stops our whole service, or the same order goes out twice.

- **What if their system is slow or broken?** Example: the lab's system is down for an hour. Can Anna still book?
- **What if we send the same thing twice?** Example: the lab gets Anna's order twice. Does she get two kits?
- **Can people see whether it worked?** Example: does the clinic see "sent to lab" and "lab received it"?
- **Who is right when the two systems disagree?** Example: our system says the kit was sent. The courier says it was not.

## Stages something goes through

**Watch for:** approve, cancel, publish, activate, close, submit, draft, pending, complete, any list of stages.

**If nobody decides:** things get stuck halfway, or jump to a stage they should never reach.

- **What are all the stages, in order?** Example: booked, confirmed, attended, results sent.
- **Can it go back a stage?** Example: can a "cancelled" booking become "booked" again?
- **Who can move it to the next stage?** Example: can the patient cancel, or only the clinic?
- **What must be true first?** Example: can a booking be "confirmed" before payment?
- **What else happens at each stage?** Example: does "cancelled" send an email, give a refund, or free up the slot?
- **Can it get stuck?** Example: a booking sits in "pending" for three weeks. Does anyone notice?

## Messages: email, text, app notification

**Watch for:** send an email, notify, remind, alert, text, SMS, letter.

**If nobody decides:** people get too many messages, the wrong message, or none at all, and nobody knows.

- **Can a person say "stop sending me these"?** Which messages must still go, like a booking confirmation?
- **What if the address is wrong?** Example: Anna typed `anna@gmial.com`. Does anyone find out?
- **What if the message cannot be delivered?** Example: Anna's inbox is full. Who finds out, and what happens next?
- **Once, or repeated?** Example: one reminder two days before, or one every day until she replies?
- **Who can see that it was sent?** Example: can the clinic check that Anna got her reminder?
- **What exactly does it say?** Who writes the words, and who approves them?
- **When does it go out?** Example: a reminder at 3:00 at night is not kind.

## Dates and times

**Watch for:** date, time, deadline, expiry, valid until, schedule, every week, history of changes.

**If nobody decides:** appointments show at the wrong hour, and limits reset on the wrong day.

- **Whose clock?** Example: Anna is in New York and books a London clinic for 10:00. Whose 10:00?
- **Which date should we use?** Example: on Monday a manager records a change that started last Friday. Does it count from Monday or Friday?
- **Can people enter a past date?** Example: can a receptionist add yesterday's walk-in patient? Who is allowed?
- **What happens when a year or billing period ends?** Example: a limit of 2 tests a year. Anna has one on 31 December and one on 1 January. Is that two in one year?
- **Weekends and bank holidays?** Example: "results within 3 days" from a Friday. Is that Monday or Wednesday?

## Lists, search, and filters

**Watch for:** list, search, filter, sort, find, browse, report, export.

**If nobody decides:** the list works with 20 items and breaks with 20,000, or hides the one item someone needs.

- **How many items can there be?** Example: 20, or 20,000? Does the person scroll or turn pages?
- **What order?** Example: newest first, or by surname?
- **Does it show deleted or old items?** Example: does a search for "Anna" find her cancelled bookings?
- **How fresh must it be?** Example: a booking made one second ago. Must it appear at once, or is a few minutes fine?
- **What can people filter by?** Example: by clinic, by date, by doctor?

## Tests, samples, and results

**Watch for:** test, sample, kit, lab, result, screening, letter, employer screening.

**If nobody decides:** a patient misses an urgent result, or an employer sees health information they must never see.

These rules often need a clinical decision. If the person cannot decide, ask who can, such as the medical director. Never invent a clinical rule.

- **What if the sample has no readable name?** Example: Anna's kit arrives with a smudged label. What happens before the lab uses it?
- **What if the sample cannot be tested?** Example: the lab says Anna's sample is spoiled. Who contacts her, and what happens next?
- **Who acts on a worrying result, and how fast?** Example: Anna's result needs a doctor within 24 hours. Who must act, and who checks it happened?
- **What may the employer see?** Example: Anna's employer paid for her test. May they see that she took it? Her result? Only totals for the whole company?
- **What if a result changes?** Example: the lab corrects Anna's result after she has read it. Who tells her, and how?

## Adding a new topic

When the same kind of surprise keeps coming back, add a new topic here. Each topic needs:

1. **Watch for** — the words that show the topic is present.
2. **If nobody decides** — one line on what goes wrong.
3. **Three to nine questions**, one decision each, with an everyday example.
